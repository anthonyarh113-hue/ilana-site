Appealing a Ministry of Interior refusal

Written by Adv. Ilana Unger, updated 18.11.2026

A refusal is not necessarily the end of the road. Many refusals stem from missing documents, inconsistencies at the interview or reasons that can be addressed. But time is limited — so act quickly and methodically.

What to do when a refusal arrives

  1. Read the decision carefully: the reasons and the deadline set for an objection or appeal.
  2. Gather everything: the application, the interview record if any, and the correspondence.
  3. Choose the route: a request for reconsideration, an objection, or an appeal to the Appeals Tribunal under the Entry into Israel Law.

The Appeals Tribunal

Appeals against Population Authority decisions on entry and status go to the Appeals Tribunal. A strong appeal rests on the facts, new evidence where available, and the relevant procedures and case law. In some cases a further petition to the administrative court is possible.

Deadlines

Every step has a time limit, usually stated in the decision letter. Missing it can close the door on an appeal — so send me the decision as early as possible.

  • Representing you in the proceedings

FAQ

How long do I have to appeal?

It depends on the type of decision and is usually stated in the refusal letter. Don’t wait — send the decision for review right away.

Can I stay in Israel during the appeal?

It depends on your circumstances and current status. Interim relief can sometimes be requested — check early.

Does an appeal guarantee a result?

No. The decision lies with the authorities and the tribunal. I tell you honestly what the prospects are before we start.
