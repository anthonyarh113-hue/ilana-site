Graduated procedure for married spouses

Written by Adv. Ilana Unger, updated 25.11.2026

Married to an Israeli citizen, with a spouse who is not Israeli? The road to status runs through the Population and Immigration Authority’s graduated procedure — several years of renewable visas and checks that the relationship is genuine. I guide couples through every stage.

What the graduated procedure is

It is the process through which a foreign spouse gains status in Israel step by step. Usually a temporary B/1 visa with a work permit comes first, then temporary residency (A/5) with an Israeli ID and social rights, and finally citizenship. At each stage the Authority checks that the marriage is genuine and that the couple’s centre of life is in Israel.

How long it takes

The procedure takes several years. Exact stages and timeframes are set by the Authority’s procedures, which are updated from time to time, and depend on your circumstances — for example whether the Israeli spouse is a citizen or a permanent resident. At the consultation we map out a realistic timeline for your case.

Documents usually required

  • IDs and valid passports of both spouses
  • Marriage certificate and proof of its registration
  • Birth certificates and civil-status documents — often apostilled with a notarised translation
  • Police clearance from the country of origin
  • Evidence of a shared life: lease, joint accounts, photos, correspondence

The exact list depends on the country and the stage — this is where small mistakes cause long delays.

FAQ

Do we have to live in Israel during the procedure?

Yes. One condition is that the couple’s centre of life is in Israel, and the Authority checks this throughout.

What happens if we separate mid-way?

The procedure is usually stopped. In some cases — for example with joint children or after a substantial part of the procedure — status may be requested on humanitarian grounds.

Can the foreign spouse work at the start?

In most cases the first visa includes a work permit. It is worth checking which visa was actually issued.
