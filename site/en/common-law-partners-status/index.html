Status for common-law partners

Written by Adv. Ilana Unger, updated 25.11.2026

Living together without a registered marriage, with a partner who isn’t Israeli? Common-law couples — including same-sex couples — can obtain status too, but the burden of proving the relationship is heavier. Preparation makes the difference.

Who can apply

An Israeli citizen or permanent resident who shares a life with a foreign partner without a registered marriage. Applications are examined under the Authority’s procedure for cohabiting couples, last updated in 2026.

What has to be shown

  • That the relationship is genuine and stable, not just for status
  • That you actually live together and share a household
  • That your centre of life is in Israel
  • That there is no criminal or other bar

Strong evidence includes a joint lease, accounts in both names, photos over time, correspondence and letters from family and friends.

How it differs from marriage

The common-law procedure is usually longer and more often ends in permanent residency rather than direct citizenship. If you are considering marriage, check in advance how it would affect your procedure.

How I help

  • Assessing whether there are grounds and what is missing
  • Building a convincing evidence file
  • Interview preparation — partners are questioned separately
  • Guidance through renewals, document requests and refusals

FAQ

Can same-sex couples apply?

Yes. The procedure covers same-sex couples, married or not.

How long must we live together before applying?

There is no single magic number — the Authority weighs all evidence of a stable relationship. We review your evidence at the consultation.

Can we apply if my partner is in Israel without a visa?

This is a sensitive situation that needs careful review before any filing. Contact me before approaching the Authority.
